How this was researched — methodology & conflicts ▸

Model selection

Each laptop is evaluated at its current, publicly listed base or as-tested configuration as of August 2026. Where a product line had multiple recent generations available in search results, the newest shipping model was used and the older one was set aside rather than blended in.

Price

Starting/base configuration MSRP as reported by the retailer or outlet at time of review, in USD. Sale prices and coupon-discounted prices were excluded in favor of standard list price.

Battery life

Pulled from each outlet's own lab test where available, rather than the manufacturer's marketing claim, since test methodology (brightness, workload, display panel) varies by lab.

Performance

Geekbench 6 multi-core CPU score from the specific configuration each outlet tested. This favors CPU throughput; it does not capture sustained/thermal performance, GPU-bound creative work, or Apple Silicon's efficiency-per-watt advantage.

Display quality

Unlike the other four criteria, there is no single agreed-upon "display quality" number in the industry. This score (1–10) is an editorial estimate we derived from each laptop's cited resolution, peak brightness, panel type (OLED vs IPS), color gamut coverage, and refresh rate — it is not itself a number found verbatim in a single source. Treat it as a starting point, not a lab measurement.

Conflicts we had to resolve

Dell XPS 13 naming. Dell discontinued and later revived the "XPS 13" name across very different products between 2024 and 2026 (including a $1,299+ Snapdragon model and a repositioned $599–699 budget model). We used the current 2026 budget-positioned model (DX13260) since it's what's actually for sale today, and flagged this so numbers aren't confused with the older, pricier XPS 13 generation.
Dell XPS 13 battery life. Tom's Guide measured 14 hours 30 minutes in its own test; Engadget measured 15 hours 56 minutes using PCMark 10's Modern Office benchmark on a different configuration. We display Tom's Guide's figure and note the spread here rather than averaging them into a number neither outlet actually measured.
ThinkPad X1 Carbon battery life. Multiple reviewers flagged Gen 12's battery as a weak point specifically on the OLED display configuration (ITPro measured 10 hours 29 minutes); a lower-resolution IPS configuration would likely last longer, but we couldn't find a directly tested figure for that specific panel, so the OLED figure is used and labeled.
Zenbook 14 battery life. Results vary hugely by workload: PCWorld recorded roughly 17 hours under light/low-brightness use but just 3 hours 30 minutes under sustained high load, while Laptop Mag's real-world mixed-use test landed near 16 hours. We used Laptop Mag's real-world figure as the representative number and flagged the wider range.

What this comparison does not capture

Keyboard feel, trackpad quality, port selection, repairability, webcam quality, speaker quality, and long-term reliability all came up repeatedly in the underlying reviews but aren't reduced to a number here — they're worth reading the linked reviews for directly.
