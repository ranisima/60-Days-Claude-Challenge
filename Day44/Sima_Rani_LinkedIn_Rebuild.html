Day 4Comment on 5 posts in your target space▸
The Value Comment formula: Agree + Add Insight + Ask a Question.
  • Agree — briefly say what part of their point lands for you (one sentence, specific, not "Great post!")
  • Add Insight — bring something from your own experience/perspective they didn't say
  • Ask a Question — invite a reply, which is what actually gets you seen by their audience

Target posts from small-agency owners, freelance devs, and startup founders talking about websites, hiring, or freelancing — that's where your future clients are reading.

Day 5Publish Post #2 — lessons-learned / hot take▸
Unpopular opinion: most small business websites don't need to be "impressive." They need to load in under 2 seconds and tell a visitor exactly what to do next. I've seen too many sites where the developer optimized for looking clever instead of being clear — animations that slow the page down, navigation that needs a tutorial, copy buried under a hero video. Speed and clarity aren't the boring choice. They're the choice that actually gets a visitor to become a customer. What's the most "impressive but useless" website feature you've seen?
≈ 660 characters
Day 6Engage with every comment + 5 more connections▸

Reply to every single comment on both posts within a few hours — this is what LinkedIn's algorithm rewards most, and it's also literally client-facing behavior you're demonstrating in public. Send 5 more targeted connection requests using the Day 3 approach.

Day 7Review profile views & connection acceptance rate▸

Profile views up = your headline/keywords are getting you found in search; keep optimizing keywords over time.
Low connection-accept rate (under ~30%) = your request message or profile photo isn't building enough trust — revisit both.
Posts got views but no comments = your hook or your question at the end isn't inviting a reply — the last line of a post should always be answerable in one sentence.
Nothing moved at all = normal for week one. Consistency over 3-4 weeks is what actually compounds on LinkedIn, not one good week.
